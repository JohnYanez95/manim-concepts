\begin{narrative}
\section*{The road so far}

\roadmap{1}

\noindent You own two moves now: \emph{independent stages multiply}, and
\emph{count with too much order, then divide the surplus out}. The last
problem left you holding a number — $\anchor{001.paths15}$ routes through
a small lattice — that seemed to be about a walker.

The next chapter poses the problem this whole guide exists to solve, and
your two moves are the first tools it reaches for: the product rule sizes
a search space of $3^4 = \anchor{001.raw81}$ raw sequences in one line,
and the divide-out
instinct is what makes the constrained count $\binom{T+U}{T-U}$ readable.
The walker's lattice is about to reappear as a grid of \emph{states
against frames} — same fifteen, different costume — and the question
will stop being ``how many?'' and become ``how heavy is each one?''.
That second question is a debt the chapter takes on openly; most of this
book is the repayment.
\end{narrative}
